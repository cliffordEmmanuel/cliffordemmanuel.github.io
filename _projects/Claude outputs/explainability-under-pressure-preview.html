                In my research: a targeted attack pushed the top reason out of the top ten in about one in five network flows on one dataset, with the alert unchanged. On the other dataset, the same attack barely worked.

                What the three tests show

                1. A quick estimate can give a different reason every time you ask, even when nothing has changed. Before you read meaning into a reason, check how much it varies on its own.
                2. Tiny, random changes usually leave the reason alone, but not always.
                3. A deliberate attacker can change the reason while the alert stays the same. Random changes rarely do this. A targeted search does.

                These are three different weaknesses. A system can pass one test and fail another, so each one needs its own check. That was the central finding of my MSc research, which tested real explanation tools (SHAP and LIME) on real intrusion-detection data.

                What this is, and is not: a small, fully visible toy model with one made-up event. It runs entirely in your browser. It shows the ideas from my research at a small scale. It does not reproduce the research results, and it is not a real security system.
                For the technical reader

                Model. Risk = logistic(−6.0 + 0.22·logins + 0.06·unusual time + 0.20·data leaving + 0.39·scans + 0.08·new device + 0.044·data leaving·new device + 0.054·unusual time·logins), with every signal on a 0 to 10 scale. High risk is 70% or above; needs review is 40% to 69%.

                Reasons. Shapley values of the risk score, measured against a quiet reference event with every signal at 1. The percentages are each signal's share of the positive contributions. “Careful calculation” is exact (all 32 coalitions), as with TreeSHAP. “Quick estimate” uses permutation sampling with 5 samples, which behaves like the sampling in KernelSHAP and LIME.

                Test 2. Each signal moves by −1, 0 or +1, chosen at random.

                Test 3. The attacker may move each signal except data leaving by at most one step. An exhaustive search of all 81 combinations finds 3 that keep the alert high and change the top reason. The interaction terms make this possible: the same data volume gets less of the credit when the device signal drops and the login signals rise.

                Reproducibility. All random draws use a fixed seed, so the page gives the same sequence of results each time it loads.

                Limits. The attacker here knows the full model. My research used an attacker who could only send inputs and read the outputs. Real network signals cannot always change freely.
