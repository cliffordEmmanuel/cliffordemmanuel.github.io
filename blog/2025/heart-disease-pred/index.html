Heart-Disease Classification with Public Data: An Experiment

Created on May 30, 2025 by Clifford

2025   ·   machine learning   classification   experimentation

This was a public-dataset machine-learning exercise, not a clinical prediction system. It explored how a few standard classifiers behaved on a dataset of 1,025 anonymised records with 13 clinical features. It should not be interpreted as medical advice, a diagnostic model, or evidence of clinical performance.

The exercise

I worked through a familiar workflow: exploratory analysis, preprocessing, comparison of logistic regression, random forest, K-nearest neighbours, and an RBF-kernel support-vector machine, followed by evaluation using classification metrics and cross-validation.

Why the result needs caution

The original experiment reported a very high random-forest score of roughly 99.7%. A score that strong on a small, public benchmark is a reason to inspect the experiment carefully—not a reason to assume real-world usefulness. Potential sources of over-optimism include duplicate or related records, leakage between training and evaluation data, the split strategy, limited representativeness, or choices made during preprocessing and tuning.

I do not treat that figure as evidence that the approach would generalise to a clinical setting. Medical use would require much stronger evidence, including appropriate data governance, external validation, prospective evaluation, and clinical oversight.

What I would do differently

If revisiting the work, I would make data provenance and the evaluation protocol the centre of the write-up. I would test for duplicates and leakage before model selection, keep a genuinely untouched test set, report uncertainty rather than a single score, and discuss how error costs differ for real users.

The value of the exercise is the lesson: an attractive metric is a prompt for deeper evaluation, especially when a model could be mistaken for a high-stakes decision tool.

The original project materials are available in the heart-disease-prediction repository.
